GUIDE

Presets and Custom Models

The model's coefficients ship as named presets. You can change any settable coefficient at runtime, and the fingerprint makes sure a changed model can never pass as the shipped one.

Reasons to Touch the Model

People change the model for three reasons: they want a calmer or wilder market for a stress study, they are studying the model itself one coefficient at a time, or they are calibrating against their own data. Even if you change nothing, you still quote the preset name, because it is part of what lets someone else rerun your result.

Presets

A preset names the complete coefficient set, frozen and documented. pt-v3 is the current default; pt-v1 and pt-v2 stay selectable and bit-reproducing forever. The reason is comparability: (package version, model, universe fingerprint, seed) is a complete, citable specification of a market. If every user ran a bespoke coefficient set, "tested on the pretium simulator" would mean nothing.

PYTHON 
eng = pt.Engine(seed=42, universe=u, model="pt-v3")  # the default, spelled out
eng = pt.Engine(seed=42, universe=u, model="pt-v1")  # an earlier era, still exact

Three presets ship. All three stay selectable and bit-reproducing forever.

PresetWhat it isBehaviour to know about
pt-v1 The original reference model, ported from the engine this library grew out of. Uncalibrated. Strong herding (momentum_theta 0.25), so returns trend hard: autocorrelation +0.249 at lag one against a real band around zero. Momentum strategies look brilliant here for that reason. No GJR term, so no leverage effect. A constant market-factor sigma, so cross-sectional correlation sits near +0.024 against a real +0.08 to +0.56.
pt-v2 The first calibrated preset, fitted against the re-derived realism bands. Selectable, never the default. Carries the era's structural changes (GJR asymmetry, a conditional-variance market factor, VIX coupling) at the first converged coefficient set. Stronger scenario response than pt-v3: it retains more of pt-v1's shock transient, at the cost of weaker volatility clustering.
pt-v3 The current default, and the one the realism numbers describe. Nine of ten statistics in band at 252 days. Herding turned well down (momentum_theta 0.0742), so return autocorrelation drops to +0.0375. Factor-variance persistence raised to 0.989 to buy clustering, which is what weakened the crisis transient to 27.6% of pt-v1's. Idiosyncratic noise scaled to 0.84 to fund the bigger market factor.
pt-v4 Selectable, and deliberately not the default. pt-v3 plus nine parameters that ship inert on pt-v3: five endogenous jump coefficients, three volume-process parameters, and market_factor_sigma. Built for long horizons. It halves the dual-horizon objective (0.9887 to 0.4863 on training seeds, 1.3990 to 0.7493 on thirty seeds it never saw) and is the first change in the project's history to move the thin-tails gap: excess kurtosis at 504 days goes from 5.23 to 9.19, inside the 7.1 to 22 band. The cost is at the certified horizon, where it holds 8 of 10 rather than 9, having surrendered return_acf1.
pt-v5 Selectable, not the default. pt-v4 with the jump decoupled from the herding term, via jump_momentum_share. pt-v4's trade was a wiring accident rather than a law. A jump was applied to the mispricing state at the day close, after the momentum roll had already recorded the pre-jump level, so at the next close herding read the jump as a re-rating and continued it. Fattening the tail and adding return continuation were the same write to the same variable, which is why no search over those coefficients escaped it. Separating them recovers return_acf1 at the certified horizon: 9 of 10 at 252 days and 7 of 10 at 504.
pt-v6 Selectable, not the default. pt-v5 with the herding term halved, momentum_theta 0.0742 to 0.0371. Holds 11 of 13 at 252 days and 10 of 13 at 504 on the thirteen-statistic panel (9 of the original 10 and 8, before the conditional correlation statistics were added). pt-v3 manages 11 and 6. Halving herding brings two-year return autocorrelation into band, 0.0605 to 0.0346 against a ceiling of 0.04, and no other statistic changes band at either horizon. It is not strictly better than pt-v3: at the certified horizon the two tie on count, and pt-v3 sits more comfortably inside its bands on six of the ten, while pt-v6 buys one large gain, kurtosis room 0.80 to 7.20 seed-sd. Choose it for multi-year work where the tail matters.
pt-v7 Selectable, not the default. pt-v6 with industries: sector_factor_sigma 0.002 to 0.012, the crisis blend taken from the idiosyncratic term instead of the sector draw, a quarter of sector variance following VIX, idio_sigma_scale trimmed ten percent, crisis_blend_cap raised to 0.98, and market_vol_ceiling_multiple 8 to 16. The first preset in which names in the same sector co-move more than names in different ones, in calm markets and in a crisis. Twelve of thirteen in band at 252 days and twelve at 504, the only miss the structural volume-change row; pt-v6 holds eleven and ten. Sector excess correlation 0.128 against a real band of 0.11 to 0.23, and +0.079 in a held VIX 45 against the real 2020 window's +0.103, with crisis correlation 0.61 against pt-v6's 0.60. The crisis volatility lever reads 3.06x against pt-v6's 2.68x, because a quarter of the sector variance scales with stress, and calm volatility is lower than the base because the trim paid for it. Passed the response instrument, held-out seeds and universe, and the section 8 overfitting control. Calibration record sections 58 to 62.

Custom Models and Fingerprints

PYTHON 
custom = pt.ModelParams.from_preset("pt-v1", garch_alpha=0.12)
eng = pt.Engine(seed=42, universe=u, model=custom)
eng.model_fingerprint     # "custom-0c04c4ba" -- never "pt-v1"

The fingerprint is the first 8 hex characters of a sha256 over the canonical serialisation: names sorted, values as raw IEEE-754 bit patterns. A coefficient set bit-identical to a shipped preset fingerprints as that preset's name. Anything else fingerprints as custom-XXXXXXXX. ModelParams is immutable once built, so a fingerprint someone quoted cannot drift afterwards. The rule is simple: you may change the model, and a changed model has a different name.

The fingerprint travels everywhere a result does: scorecards, manifests, checkpoints, forks, replay. A manifest for a custom-model run embeds the full coefficient dictionary and reproduce() rebuilds that exact market or refuses by name.

Comparing Presets Fairly

Nothing settable may change how many random draws are taken or in what order. Market hours, the 390-tick day, the calendar and the sector key order are the draw schedule, and a preset changes what the draws are multiplied into, never the schedule. Run the same seed under two models and the draw counts match, so every difference in the outcome is a parameter effect, never reshuffled noise.

What Is Settable

pt.ModelParams.settable() lists the runtime surface: the two variance processes (per-name GJR-GARCH and the market factor's), the factor sigmas and idiosyncratic scale, the mispricing dynamics, the news and flow coefficients, and the guards in the tick chain. Guards are worst-case guarantees rather than tuning knobs, and a calibration search excludes them.

The rest of the surface, such as fair-value coefficients and the macro chain's constants, is visible in to_dict() and covered by the fingerprint, and overriding it is refused by name: those constants are compile-time in this build, and accepting an override the engine would ignore would make the fingerprint a lie. Two coefficients are derived: mispricing_phi and s_phi_tick come from mispricing_half_life_days and cannot be set directly.

Example: Calibrating Your Own Market

A model builder wants a market with stronger volatility clustering for a stress study, and wants the result to be citable. The loop: sweep candidate coefficients, score each candidate's realism panel, confirm the pick on fresh seeds, then publish the fingerprint beside the result.

PYTHON 
from pretium import facts, loss

for alpha in (0.02, 0.04, 0.08):
    params = pt.ModelParams.from_preset("pt-v3", garch_alpha=alpha)
    panels = [facts.measure(seed=s, universe=u, model=params)
              for s in range(1, 7)]
    result = loss.band_distance_loss(panels)
    print(params.fingerprint, result["loss"])

band_distance_loss prices each statistic's band exit in units of its own seed noise and returns the full per-statistic breakdown with the scalar inside it. The structurally unreachable statistic rides along in every result as a standing falsification verdict, and stays out of the loss, because an optimiser pointed at an unreachable target distorts every other parameter chasing it.

When the sweep has more than a handful of knobs, survey first: Atlas tells you which parameters move your panel before you spend a search on them, and confirm re-measures the winner on seeds the sweep never saw. The library consumes presets and does not ship an optimiser; six consecutive searches on this simulator were rejected for trades a scalar objective made silently, and that history is why.

Ship the result as (package version, custom-XXXXXXXX, universe fingerprint, seed) plus a RunManifest. A reader with the package and the manifest rebuilds your market bit for bit, or is told exactly why not. Citing a Result has the full list and a worked methods paragraph.

Turning One Knob Down

One coefficient, honestly named, all the way to a citable result. The library's best-known defect is that returns trend (+0.249 autocorrelation at lag one under pt-v1). One knob controls it, and turning it down is a two-line experiment:

PYTHON 
custom = pt.ModelParams.from_preset("pt-v1", momentum_theta=0.05)
custom.fingerprint            # "custom-..." -- never "pt-v1"

eng = pt.Engine(seed=42, universe=u, model=custom)
panel = pt.facts.measure(seed=1, universe=u, model=custom)
panel["return_acf1"]
# measured for this counterfactual: +0.034, inside the real band
# (-0.08, 0.06), where pt-v1 reads +0.249. Volatility and kurtosis
# essentially unchanged. Measured on an earlier model era; re-measure
# before leaning on it.

manifest = pt.RunManifest.of(eng, seed=42, universe=u)
open("run.json", "w").write(manifest.to_json())

The pieces working together: the coefficient set is immutable, the fingerprint says loudly that this is not the shipped model, the panel re-measures realism under it instead of inheriting the certificate, and the manifest hands a reader everything needed to rebuild the exact market and check you.
