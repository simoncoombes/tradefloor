API

Shipped Presets

A preset is a complete, named, frozen set of model coefficients. Name one when you build an engine, or pass nothing and get the default.

PYTHON 
eng = pt.Engine(seed=42, universe=u)                  # pt-v12, the default
eng = pt.Engine(seed=42, universe=u, model="pt-v12")  # the same, spelled out
eng = pt.Engine(seed=42, universe=u, model="pt-v1")   # an earlier era, still exact
eng = pt.Engine(seed=42, universe=u, model="pt-v4")   # for multi-year questions

The Shipped Presets

pt-v1The original reference model, ported from the engine this library grew out of. Herding runs strong (momentum_theta 0.25), which is what made momentum so profitable in early eras. Kept selectable and bit-reproducing forever, so old results stay checkable.
pt-v2The first calibrated preset, produced by the calibration tooling against the re-derived realism bands. Selectable, never the default. Its certificate and report are committed in the repository.
pt-v3The default until pt-v10 replaced it at 0.2.0, produced the same way, and the preset the realism numbers described then: nine of the ten statistics measured at the time were in band at 252 days. Herding is turned well down (momentum_theta 0.0742).
pt-v4Selectable, and deliberately not the default. pt-v3 plus nine parameters that ship inert on pt-v3: five endogenous jump coefficients, three volume-process parameters, and market_factor_sigma. Built to investigate horizons past a year, which is not the same as being certified for them: nothing in this project is certified past 252 days.

Choosing Between pt-v3 and pt-v4

A historical choice, kept because work published against either preset still reproduces. Neither is the default: pt-v12 has been since 2026-08-26, and it holds all fourteen statistics at 252 days AND all fourteen at 504, so it dominates both rows below. The full table of the twelve shipped presets is on Presets and Custom Models.

The two counts below are out of the ten-statistic panel of the pt-v3 era, and the two horizons disagree.

Horizonpt-v3pt-v4
252 days (certified)9/10 in band8/10
504 days5/107/10

Between these two, and only these two: pt-v3 at or under a year, pt-v4 for INVESTIGATING longer horizons. Neither licenses a multi-year backtest, which the realism page forbids on every preset, and pt-v12 beats both at both horizons.

What pt-v4 buys is the thin-tails gap, which nothing before it had moved: excess kurtosis at 504 days goes from 5.23 to 9.19, inside the horizon-matched band of 7.1 to 22. It halves the dual-horizon objective, 0.9887 to 0.4863 on training seeds and 1.3990 to 0.7493 on thirty seeds the search never saw.

What it costs is return_acf1 at the certified horizon: 0.0739 on training seeds, 0.0838 on held-out seeds, and 0.0801 on a held-out 60-name universe, against a band ceiling of 0.06. Three independent axes agreeing makes that a regression rather than seed noise.

That is why it is not the default. Promoting it would turn the envelope's nine-of-ten into eight-of-ten, and the statistic it gives up is the one behind "momentum works here for the wrong reason", which is the defect pt-v3 exists to have fixed.

Why Presets Exist

So results can be compared. Package version, model, universe fingerprint and seed pin a market down completely, so two papers quoting pt-v3 ran the same coefficients to the bit. If everyone ran their own, "tested on the pretium simulator" would mean nothing. Presets never change once shipped. A recalibration gets a new name, and the old names keep producing exactly what they always did.

Reading a Preset

pt.model_preset()The compact coefficient table for the default, in its exact historical shape. The cross-platform determinism gate hashes every value in it.
ModelParams.from_preset(name)The full surface as an object: read any coefficient, or override some and get an honestly named custom model.
engine.model_fingerprintWhat the engine is actually running: a preset's name, or custom-XXXXXXXX. A modified set can never present as a shipped preset, and a set bit-identical to a preset fingerprints as the preset.

The workflow for changing coefficients, and why the fingerprint rule exists, is the guide chapter Presets and custom models. What each coefficient does is Model parameters.
