API

Model Parameters

Every runtime-settable coefficient and what it controls. pt.ModelParams.settable() lists this surface from your installed wheel; this page explains it.

Two rules hold everywhere. Nothing settable changes how many random draws happen or in what order, so two coefficient sets on one seed see identical noise and every difference in the outcome is a parameter effect. And several newer mechanisms ship at 0.0, where the code skips the branch entirely, so earlier presets reproduce bit for bit.

The Shared Factors

Every return has three parts: a move the whole market shares, a move the sector shares, and the name's own private noise. These set the size of each part.

market_factor_sigmaHow big the shared market-wide move is on a normal day. Every stock feels this one.
sector_factor_sigmaThe same, for each sector's shared move.
idio_sigma_scaleScales each name's own private volatility. 1.0 leaves it untouched. pt-v3 pays for a bigger market factor by setting this to 0.84.

Order Flow and News

order_flow_coefficientHow hard aggregate order flow pushes the price.
informed_flow_fractionThe share of that push that sticks. The rest fades back.
news_sector_weight · news_market_weightHow much sector-wide and market-wide news events move each name.
news_peer_weight · news_peer_weight_downHow much one company's news moves its sector peers. At 0.0 an earnings beat moves only the company that reported it. Bad news gets its own knob because it spreads harder than good news in real markets.

Per-Name Volatility: GJR-GARCH

The recipe behind volatility clustering: a big move today makes a big move tomorrow more likely. Each name runs its own copy.

garch_omegaThe base level variance returns toward.
garch_alphaHow much yesterday's shock raises today's variance. Bigger alpha, jumpier volatility.
garch_betaHow much of yesterday's variance carries over. Bigger beta, longer memory.
garch_gammaThe extra kick after a down day. This is where the leverage effect comes from. Zero gives plain symmetric GARCH, bit for bit.
garch_ceiling_multiple · garch_floor_multipleBounds on the variance, as multiples of the sector's long-run level. The ceiling binds on clustering, so it is searched under bounds.

The Market Factor's Variance

The shared factor has its own variance process, and VIX steers it. This is why crisis volatility and crisis correlation arrive together.

market_vol_alpha · market_vol_betaShock take-up and persistence. pt-v3 runs the persistence at 0.989 to buy clustering, and that one choice is what weakened the crisis transient (gap 5).
market_vol_vix_couplingHow much the variance target follows VIX. 0 ignores it; 1 makes the target proportional to VIX².
market_vol_vix_anchorThe VIX level where the coupled target equals baseline. Ships at 15, so an average VIX reproduces the uncoupled process exactly.
market_vol_ceiling_multiple · market_vol_floor_multipleClamps on the factor variance. The 8x ceiling is why pinning VIX above about 42 buys almost no extra volatility.
market_vol_slow_persistence · market_vol_slow_gain · market_vol_slow_weightA second, slower variance component: the fast one chases spikes, this one carries long-horizon clustering. All three at 0.0 recover the single-component process bit for bit.
market_vol_slow_vix_dampDecouples the slow component from VIX. Measured, and the measurement refuted the idea behind it: damping makes both the shock response and the steady-state lever worse at every setting tried. Ships inert.

Crisis Behaviour

crisis_vix_thresholdThe VIX level where crisis behaviour switches on: the correlation blend, the stress memory, the economy's crisis premium. Ships at 25.5, the 94th percentile of the endogenous VIX distribution, chosen so the trigger can actually fire.
crisis_blend_ramp · crisis_blend_capHow many VIX points past the threshold before correlations top out, and where the ceiling sits.
crash_amplifier_threshold · crash_amplifier_slopeHow big a market shock has to be (in normal-day units) before every name loads harder on the market, and how much harder per extra unit.
universe_stress_decay · universe_stress_weightFear memory. Without it, correlations decouple the same tick VIX dips back under the threshold. With it, stress ratchets up instantly and fades over weeks, the way real crisis correlation does.
regime_stress_pointsStress the business cycle feeds into that same memory, in VIX-equivalent points. At 0.0 the price process behaves as though the economy were always expanding.
vix_mean_reversionHow fast VIX falls back toward its target each day. The other half of the transient problem: a longer spike stresses the market without touching variance persistence.

Jumps

Without jumps, prices only diffuse, and nothing ever gaps. That is why 504-day kurtosis reads 5.2 against a real 7.1 to 22: fat tails at that scale need surprises. Jumps draw from their own RNG stream, so a preset with them off is bit-identical to one that predates them.

jump_intensity_market · jump_sigma_marketDaily probability and size of a market-wide jump.
jump_mean_marketNegative on purpose: real crash jumps point down. A symmetric jump would fix kurtosis on the panel while getting crises backwards.
jump_intensity_idio · jump_sigma_idioPer-name jumps, independent across names: the earnings-surprise channel.

Volume

volume_variance_gainHow strongly realised volume tracks the market factor's variance, so busy prices come with busy volume.
volume_persistence · volume_innovation_sigmaA shared day-to-day volume process, so a busy day is followed by a busy day. This is the machinery aimed at the one structurally failed statistic: without a volume process, day-to-day volume changes are pure noise and their autocorrelation sits near −0.5 at any coefficients.

Size Effects

Small companies are more volatile and cost more to trade. The original model did this in four hard steps, which put cliffs in the cross-section: a $49B company and a $51B company differed by 25% in volatility. These blend the steps into smooth curves.

size_effect_smoothness · size_effect_exponentBlend from the four-tier volatility step toward a continuous power law (0 = steps, 1 = pure curve), and the curve's slope. The exponent ships at 0.15, fitted to the steps where the steps make sense.
spread_size_smoothness · spread_size_exponentThe same for the bid-ask spread, where the steps were worse: 10 bps at $1.0B, 30 bps at $0.9B. The exponent ships at 0.455, fitted the same way.

Mispricing Dynamics

The wedge between price and true value: how far it stretches, how fast it heals, and how much the crowd chases it. This is the layer the truth table records.

mispricing_half_life_daysTrading days for half a mispricing to fade. The one knob for the decay: setting it recomputes the two derived values below.
mispricing_phi · s_phi_tickDerived from the half-life (daily and per-tick decay). Carried as recorded bit patterns; you cannot set them directly.
momentum_thetaHerding: the fraction of yesterday's re-rating that continues today. The knob behind trending returns. At pt-v1’s 0.25 it measures +0.249 at lag one; pt-v3 ships 0.0742 and measures +0.0375, inside the real band of −0.08 to 0.06. A counterfactual at 0.05 measured +0.034 on an earlier model era.
crowd_valuation_gain · crowd_momentum_gainHow hard the crowd leans on cheapness and on yesterday's move.

Guards

Worst-case guarantees. Settable, so you can study them, and excluded from any calibration search, because a guard tuned for realism stops being a guarantee.

mispricing_capHard bound on how far price can stretch from value.
crowd_lean_capBound on the crowd's daily push.
price_breaker_fractionThe circuit-breaker band around the session open. Ships at ±25%.
price_hard_capAbsolute cap on any model price. Ships at 50,000.

To find out which of these move an outcome you care about, and by how much, survey them with Atlas before you turn anything. To set one: pt.ModelParams.from_preset("pt-v3", garch_alpha=0.12), which fingerprints as custom. The rest of the preset surface (fair-value coefficients, the macro chain's constants, the book geometry) is visible in to_dict() and refused by name if you try to set it, because those constants are compiled in and a silently ignored override would make the fingerprint a lie.
