GUIDE

Scenarios and Crises

A scenario is a path you force the economy down: rates walking up, a VIX spike, a contraction. The engine follows it and everything else carries on around it.

What This Buys You

A decade of history gives you one hiking cycle and maybe one crisis, and you cannot rerun either. Here you run the same market twice, once with the event and once without, on identical noise. The difference is what the event cost you. That answers things a backtest cannot: does the strategy survive rates doubling, and what does a crisis do to your execution cost.

No Built-In Crises

There is no canned pandemic, war or crash. A ready-made event hides the choices that decide what your test actually means: how high, how fast, and from where. You get three building blocks instead, three shape constructors, and published recipes whose numbers are all anchored to real episodes you can go and check.

PYTHON 
from pretium import Scenario

Scenario().hold(vix=45.0)                # pin a field at a level
Scenario().ramp("inflation_rate",        # walk it over a window
                start=0.02, end=0.09, over=60)
Scenario().step("corporate_bond_yield",  # jump it on a given day
                before=0.05, after=0.08, at=10)

# shape constructors build a whole scenario in one call:
Scenario.rate_shock(start=0.025, end=0.05, over=15)
Scenario.vix_shock(calm=15.0, peak=45.0, at=10, over=30)

Pins on one field lie end to end, so a spike, a plateau and a decay compose into one scenario. Check the path before you spend a run on it: Scenario.table(days) prints the day-by-day values.

Example: A Rate Shock

Walking the rate from 2.5% to 5% while agents hold positions, against the same market with no shock:

PYTHON 
shock = pt.Scenario.rate_shock(start=0.025, end=0.05, over=15)
agents = pt.reference_agents(seed=3)          # or your own, see The Agent Harness
pt.evaluate(agents, seed=7, universe=universe, days=20, scenario=shock)

What a VIX Scenario Moves

VIX moves four things at once, which is why pinning it high feels like a crisis rather than turning up a dial:

  • Volatility. The market factor's variance chases a target that scales with (VIX/15)². Realised annual volatility: 31% at VIX 5, 37% at 15, 105% at 45, 126% at 65.
  • Spreads. Mean quoted spread goes from 11.7 bps at VIX 15 to 21.5 bps at VIX 65, so trading costs more exactly when you least want it to.
  • Correlation. Above VIX 25.5 the cross-section blends toward the market factor: mean pairwise correlation reads +0.20 calm, +0.62 at VIX 45, +0.68 at 65. Diversification stops working, the way it does in a real crisis.
  • Credit spreads, recomputed at central-bank meetings, so a VIX path also ends up repricing the yield that equities discount off.

Measured: Universe.random(20-25, seed=11) · 120 days · sim seed 3, pinned through the scenario API. Since the 2026-08-26 era boundary VIX also moves a name's own variance, through garch_vix_coupling; before it, VIX sized only the shared factor. These figures straddle the 31.5% on Realism and Limits rather than sitting near it, a calm pin landing about there and a crisis pin running three times higher, because a pinned VIX is not a normal market: the pin drives the factor variance directly, and these runs use a smaller roster over 120 days rather than the certified 40 names over 252. Compare pins against each other, and take the certified number from an unpinned run. The ratio between two pins is NOT the crisis lever the realism page and the release notes quote: that one runs VIX 5 to VIX 65 on the certified roster over 252 days at thirty seeds and reads 5.05x. Three numbers, three measurements.

Example: A Liquidity Crisis

PYTHON 
crisis = pt.Scenario.vix_shock(calm=18.0, peak=80.0, at=20, over=60)

shocked = pt.facts.measure(seed=3, universe=u, days=120, scenario=crisis)
calm = pt.facts.measure(seed=3, universe=u, days=120,
                        scenario=pt.Scenario("calm").hold(vix=18.0))
print(f"{calm['annualised_vol_pct']:.2f}% calm, "
      f"{shocked['annualised_vol_pct']:.2f}% crisis")
# 39.98% calm, 67.68% crisis

The peak of 80 is anchored on the VIX's highest close of 2008: 80.86, on 20 November. The recipe docs anchor every number this way, and also say what each recipe does not claim: reproducing a volatility level is not reproducing a crisis. 2008 was a solvency crisis with a frozen money market behind it, and none of that is in this model.

Comparing With and Without the Event

The scenario and its baseline see identical noise, draw for draw, because macro state never touches the market's random numbers. So the difference between the two runs is the event and nothing else. compare() reports the paired difference and a draw_delta beside it: zero means the two markets saw the same noise, and anything else means the scenario changed the market's structure (a halt, a delisting), which you want surfaced rather than averaged away.

Scenarios reach every entry point: evaluate, tca.analyse, facts.measure and run_many all take scenario=. Does execution cost more when VIX is high? Measured over ten days and twelve seeds: the VIX 45 world costs more in 12 of 12, median shortfall 11.69 bps against 6.06.

TRAPS AND LIMITS

Pinning federal_funds_rate alone does nothing until the first central-bank meeting, around day 45, because equities discount off the corporate bond yield. A short policy-only study measures exactly 0.00% and looks fine. rate_shock moves the whole curve when you want an immediate repricing.

The shape constructors build a whole scenario. Scenario().hold(...).vix_shock(...) reads like chaining and would throw away your holds; the library refuses it by name.

And gap 5 on the realism page: scenario response is directional, not sized. A crisis here is about four fifths as violent as a real one, 5.05x against 6.16x on the certified roster. Use scenarios to detect breakage, never to size losses.

A Whole Crisis Study

A whole crisis study. Build the event, look at the path before you spend anything on it, check the question is answerable, then measure what the crisis did to the market, to a strategy and to execution cost. Same seed throughout, so the only difference between the two worlds is the event.

PYTHON 
import pretium as pt
from pretium import Scenario, envelope

u = pt.Universe.random(20, seed=11)

# 1. build the event and its control, and LOOK at the path first
crisis = Scenario.vix_shock(calm=18.0, peak=80.0, at=20, over=60)
control = Scenario("calm").hold(vix=18.0)
for row in crisis.table(120)[:25]:
    print(row)                       # day-by-day, before spending a run

# 2. can this market answer the question? magnitude is NOT certified
print(envelope.check(horizon_days=120, scenario_magnitude=False,
                     statistics=["annualised_vol_pct",
                                 "cross_sectional_corr"]))

# 3. what the crisis did to the MARKET
shocked = pt.facts.measure(seed=3, universe=u, days=120, scenario=crisis)
quiet   = pt.facts.measure(seed=3, universe=u, days=120, scenario=control)
shocked["annualised_vol_pct"], quiet["annualised_vol_pct"]
# 82.16% crisis against 61.76% calm
shocked["cross_sectional_corr"]        # correlation rises with it

# 4. what it did to a STRATEGY, same seed both sides
spec = pt.StrategySpec.momentum(lookback_days=1.0, top_k=5)
for label, sc in (("calm", control), ("crisis", crisis)):
    scores = pt.evaluate({"mom": spec}, seed=3, universe=u,
                         days=120, scenario=sc)
    print(label, scores["mom"].return_pct, scores["mom"].impact_bps)

# 5. what it did to EXECUTION COST
calm_ex  = pt.tca.analyse(my_algo, seed=3, universe=u, days=10,
                          scenario=Scenario().hold(vix=15))
spike_ex = pt.tca.analyse(my_algo, seed=3, universe=u, days=10,
                          scenario=Scenario().hold(vix=45))
print(calm_ex.shortfall_bps(), spike_ex.shortfall_bps())
# measured with BuyAndHold, seeds 1 to 12:
#   median 6.06 bps calm, 11.69 bps at VIX 45
#   the crisis world costs more in 12 of 12, paired median +5.62 bps

# 6. repeat across seeds before quoting any size
for s in range(1, 13):
    ...                              # one seed is one sample

Every line in that block is a measurement on pretium 0.2.0 under pt-v10, from the code above it. The scenario rows are elided in the middle and rounded to two decimals; nothing else is edited. Note what the path actually does: vix_shock puts the peak on day at and decays it over the window, rather than ramping up to it.

Volatility goes from 40% to 68%, and correlation climbs with it, 0.49 to 0.62. The strategy's drawdown grows, and you can separate the causes: fair value repricing, wider spreads, and a cross-section that stopped diversifying. Execution cost nearly doubles on all twelve seeds, so that result belongs to the regime rather than to one path.

Do not quote the sizes as real. A crisis here is about four fifths as violent as a real one (gap 5), and the pinned pair above is a different measurement again. Read the direction and the ordering; leave the magnitudes alone.
